# Phase 51 — Newton Lab Research Atlas and Discovery Roadmap

## Executive summary

Newton Lab has grown from a tested collection of physical solvers into a set of reusable analysis methods and controlled studies. The strongest demonstrated capabilities are: distinct numerical solver families with explicit result contracts; analytical and numerical checks for oscillator, pendulum, diffusion, and Kuramoto models; careful modal estimation and signal-detection experiments under synthetic observation limits; and reproducible protocols with frozen criteria and retained artifacts.

The evidence supports mathematics, numerical/software validation, and bounded synthetic method studies, but it does not demonstrate an independently measured application. Phases 49-50 establish model-specific trends and a conditional fold prediction; Phase 53 separated classification success from exponent-estimation failure; Phase 54 found observation-factor contrasts in synthetic data; Phase 55 found a strong low-SNR contribution to sub-5% rate error alongside a much smaller deterministic finite-amplitude signature. Phase 61 adds a distinct Kuramoto finite-size study: its high-size intervals include the continuum threshold but are too broad under frozen criteria, and point estimates straddle the reference. Exponent identifiability remained distinct and failed at every sub-5% level.

The most consequential application gap remains independent target evidence: Phase 52 found no qualified engineering system. Phases 54-55 localize how tested observation settings affect synthetic identifiability and rate error, but do not establish a real-system mechanism. The Phase 56 audit confirms that Phase 55 is consistent with a substantial noise/SNR contribution and a smaller deterministic amplitude signature, but cannot assign unique causal shares. Phase 56 recommends a compact noise-dose and estimator-form comparison; that is a research decision, not an experiment already run. Phase 32 remains `BLOCKED_AUTHORIZATION`.

## Scope and audit method

This atlas was initially updated after Phase 56 inspected Phase 48-55 reports, implementations, focused tests, protocols, and generated outputs, plus the Phase 52 candidate register. It now also incorporates Phase 60 and Phase 61 reports, protocols, model/runner implementations, focused tests, and manifested outputs. The Phase 61 output manifest and isolated rerun hashes were validated; 16 Phase 60 report/protocol/source/test/output hashes matched the recorded pre-Phase-61 baseline. This is a bounded artifact audit, not a rerun of all earlier studies.

Reports exist in `docs/research/` for Phases 22-54 except Phase 34 and Phase 38. Phase 34 is represented by the inspector module and tests; no separate report was found. Foundational Phases 1-21 are represented by docs, code, tests, and selected reports. Earlier reports and datasets were not exhaustively recalculated.

Evidence labels used below:

- **A — established theory:** analytic result under stated model assumptions.
- **B — implementation/numerical validation:** software or numerical method compared with a known or independent reference.
- **C — controlled synthetic experiment:** results for a declared generated data process.
- **D — cross-model/domain hypothesis:** a proposed relationship tested between distinct models, with scope stated.
- **E — independent empirical application:** appropriately sourced real observations or a real target system.

These categories are not a ladder where one automatically implies the next. A–D evidence is not E evidence.

### Why Phase 49 and Phase 50 do not show a universal exponent

In Phase 49, the heat mode has `lambda = pi² delta_heat` under its chosen normalization, while the pendulum's small-angle envelope rate is `gamma = omega_0 delta_pendulum`. Both are linear because the corresponding model's dissipation coefficient enters that local rate linearly. Their identical fitted power does not make the states, dissipation variables, prefactors, or mechanisms identical.

In Phase 50, the target's stable equilibrium is `x*=sqrt(mu)`, and its local Jacobian is `-2x*=-2sqrt(mu)`. The exponent one-half is therefore a target-theory result. The held-out comparison meaningfully checks rate extraction and whether the theory-derived law extrapolates better than the deliberately transferred exponent-one baseline **within this synthetic target**. It is not an independent empirical discovery of the fold law: the benchmark was constructed from that normal form, with margin and equilibrium known. The result is stronger than a plot match and weaker than application validation.

The primary condition was low observation noise (`sigma=0.0005`) and the longer window (8 normalized time units). Classification criteria passed; the frozen all-class exponent criterion failed.

### What Phase 53 explains, and what remains untested

The observed diagnostics make the scaling failure concrete without assigning a single cause. In the primary condition, the transcritical group had only 26/40 valid power fits and mean estimated boundary `0.9689` versus truth `1`. At the smallest margin (`mu=0.04`), the free-exponential fit had median relative error 10.59% for equilibrium and 98.83% for rate. By comparison, fitting the correct named equation yielded median equilibrium and margin errors no greater than 2.4% in the 15 model-by-margin groups. These results support a distinction between model-informed parameter recovery and model-agnostic exponential estimation; they do not prove that the estimator choice caused the exponent failure.

Phase 53 varied noise and window and reported margin-stratified diagnostics, so poorer observation conditions and low-margin difficulty are observed associations. Its free-exponential fits had large rate error and transcritical power fits had invalid groups and a biased estimated boundary; by contrast, named-equation fits recovered equilibrium and margin more closely in this generated setting. Phase 54 then varied amplitude, equilibrium/boundary information, noise, and window in a frozen paired ablation. The tested larger amplitudes improved valid-fit rates and q/p errors, while oracle-equilibrium access improved trajectory/exponent outcomes; these are effects of the declared interventions at tested levels, not proof that nonlinear transient bias or signal-to-noise alone caused Phase 53 failures. Phase 54 did not isolate the mechanisms within amplitude effects, and its oracle modes are diagnostics rather than deployable estimators. Process noise, drift, correlated or non-Gaussian noise, missing/irregular sampling, sensor lag, uncertain control mapping, higher-dimensional mode competition, and structural model mismatch remain untested.

## Phase 56 research decision

**At fixed sampling/window and selected sub-5% amplitudes, how do observation-noise dose and local-linear versus correct-form nonlinear fitting separately affect rate error, fit validity, and exponent identifiability?**

The proposed minimum design uses the three existing normal forms, amplitudes `0.0025` and `0.04`, noise SD fractions `0`, `0.01`, and `0.05` of equilibrium, fixed `3 tau` duration and `0.1 tau` sampling, paired innovations and held-out seeds. Compare the existing free-offset exponential estimator with a prespecified correct-normal-form estimator that estimates equilibrium and margin. Treat the latter as model-informed diagnostic, not model-agnostic performance. Keep rate-fit validity, conditional rate bias/error, and exponent validity/error/coverage as separate outcomes. Freeze validity, effect and uncertainty rules before evaluation; report an indeterminate result if the signatures overlap.

This question was new relative to Phases 53-55: Phase 53 did not isolate sub-5% amplitude, Phase 54 used larger amplitudes and one estimator, and Phase 55 used one nonzero noise dose and one estimator. Phase 57 completed the experiment; its final design uses a fixed absolute time grid to avoid revealing the true recovery rate through tau-scaled timestamps. See the Phase 57 report for results and limitations. Phase 52 remains NO-GO and Phase 32 remains BLOCKED_AUTHORIZATION.

## Phase 57 result and next research question

The Phase 57 evaluation included 6,000 held-out traces and 18,000 estimator attempts. At amplitude `0.0025`, free-offset exponential mean relative absolute rate error rose from below 0.2% at zero noise to 2.61 fold, 21.95 transcritical and 7.40 pitchfork at noise fraction `0.05`, despite 100% numerical fit validity. The tail-offset method was valid in 4,969/6,000 attempts and often returned rates near zero when valid. A correct-normal-form fit had much smaller error on its matching synthetic generators, but received model-family information and is a separate diagnostic.

Rate estimation and exponent identifiability remained distinct: only 3/36 nonzero-noise model/amplitude/estimator exponent conditions met the unchanged Phase 55 p criterion. All three used the free-offset estimator at amplitude `0.04` (fold at noise `0.0025`; transcritical at `0.0025` and `0.01`). None used the tail-offset method. Zero-noise exponent rows were not scored because repeated seeds were identical and supplied no replicate uncertainty. The noise-dose and estimator interaction varied across models and amplitudes; there is no universal winner. All evidence is synthetic and conditional on the normal forms, iid Gaussian measurement noise, five margins, two amplitudes and fixed absolute grid.

**Recommended next methods question:** Can an observation-only early-window/local-linear rate estimator reduce the tail-offset bias while retaining better low-SNR behavior than the free-offset fit, when all methods use identical fixed timestamps? This addresses the specific failure observed in Phase 57 without giving an estimator the model family or true equilibrium. It remains a proposal, not authorization to start another phase. Independent engineering qualification remains NO-GO (Phase 52), and financial data use remains BLOCKED_AUTHORIZATION (Phase 32).

## Phase 61 — Kuramoto finite-size result and thread closure

Phase 61 reused the normalized truncated-Lorentzian population from Phase 60, for which the continuum formula gives \(K_c=1.7486681672\) rad/s. Its frozen design used nested populations \(N=64,256,512,1024\), eight paired seeds, a 17-point coupling grid, and 544 primary integrations, followed by 48 tighter numerical-resolution integrations. The operational transition is the maximum positive slope in the time-averaged order parameter curve; a finite-grid bracket and paired-seed bootstrap interval are reported separately. Bootstrap resamples selecting a sweep boundary are censored under the predeclared rule.

The primary N=512 and N=1024 intervals were [1.59566, 1.98911] and [1.59566, 1.85796] rad/s. Both include continuum \(K_c\), but both exceed the frozen width limit \(0.10K_c\) (0.17487 rad/s), so the agreement criterion fails. Their point estimates (1.72681 and 1.81424 rad/s) fall on opposite sides of \(K_c\), so the persistent-same-side-offset criterion also fails. The refined solver settings changed the restricted-grid estimate by zero fine-grid intervals. The frozen conclusion is **inconclusive**: no monotone approach, persistent offset, or finite-size convergence law is established. Phase 60's independent N=256 estimate differs from Phase 61's, but their bootstrap intervals overlap; because samples are unpaired, this is only descriptive.

Evidence remains mathematical under assumptions (**A**), numerical/implementation validation (**B**), and controlled synthetic simulation (**C**), with no independent application evidence (**E**).

**Kuramoto thread status: CLOSED — INCONCLUSIVE UNDER THE DECLARED PROTOCOL.** Phase 60 and Phase 61 remain completed historical phases. The bounded Phase 61 follow-up did not meet its predeclared precision criterion and established neither a persistent offset from the continuum reference nor finite-size convergence. The zero-grid-interval shift under tighter solver settings is a numerical-resolution finding; it does not prove that the transition estimate is accurate. This conclusion applies only to the declared synthetic Kuramoto model and protocol. “Inconclusive” means the experiment did not resolve the question under its criteria; it does not show that convergence is absent. Reopen this thread only for a distinct, scientifically motivated question or a materially new method, not for further parameter sweeps aimed at forcing a conclusive outcome.
