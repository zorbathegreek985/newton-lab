# Recovery-Rate Scaling and Identifiability: Research Brief

## A. Research question

The recovery-scaling thread asked how local return rates behave near stability boundaries, and whether those rates can identify the type of boundary from finite, noisy observations. Its broad motivation is that a slowing recovery can be a useful dynamical signature, but the observed signal is filtered through branch choice, perturbation size, observation duration, noise, and estimator assumptions. A fitted exponential rate is therefore not the same quantity as an identified scaling exponent, and neither alone identifies a physical mechanism.

The work proceeded from a narrow numerical convergence study (Phase 48), through model-specific theoretical/numerical comparisons and a synthetic fold prediction (Phases 49–50), to finite-observation identifiability studies (Phases 53–55) and a noise-dose/estimator comparison (Phase 57). Phase 56 synthesized the evidence; Phase 58 closed this synthetic thread for now. The experiments test declared mathematical generators. They do not establish that an independently observed system follows these laws.

## B. Mathematical models and assumptions

For a scalar system \(\dot{x}=f(x;\mu)\), an equilibrium \(x^*(\mu)\) satisfies \(f(x^*;\mu)=0\). On a locally stable branch, the linearized perturbation obeys \(\dot{\delta x}\simeq f_x(x^*;\mu)\delta x\). The positive recovery rate is \(\rho=-f_x(x^*;\mu)>0\), so infinitesimal perturbations decay approximately as \(e^{-\rho t}\). This local statement requires a stable equilibrium and sufficiently small perturbations; finite-window fits need not recover \(\rho\) without bias.

The Phase 53 normal forms use \(\mu>0\) on specific stable branches. Their branch conditions matter:

| Normal form | Tested stable branch for \(\mu>0\) | Local recovery rate | Rate scaling |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fold / saddle-node: \(\dot{x}=\mu-x^2\) | \(x^*=+\sqrt{\mu}\); the other equilibrium \(-\sqrt{\mu}\) is unstable | \(\rho=-(-2x^*)=2\sqrt{\mu}\) | \(\rho\propto\mu^{1/2}\) |
| Transcritical: \(\dot{x}=\mu x-x^2\) | Positive branch \(x^*=\mu\); the zero branch is unstable for \(\mu>0\) | \(\rho=-(\mu-2x^*)=\mu\) | \(\rho\propto\mu^1\) |
| Supercritical pitchfork: \(\dot{x}=\mu x-x^3\) | Selected positive branch \(x^*=+\sqrt{\mu}\); the negative branch is also stable | \(\rho=-(\mu-3(x^*)^2)=2\mu\) | \(\rho\propto\mu^1\) |

The corresponding positive-equilibrium scaling \(x^*\propto\mu^q\) is \(q=1/2,1,1/2\), respectively; the recovery exponent \(p\), defined by \(\rho\propto\mu^p\), is \(1/2,1,1\). These laws follow by differentiating each stated vector field on the specified branch. They do not apply indiscriminately to every branch or parameter side. The constants also differ (for example, the fold and positive pitchfork rate coefficient is 2). Phases 53–57 encode these equations in their data generators, so their experiments evaluate inference capability under known laws; they do not discover the laws.

Phase 49 treated different, model-specific coordinates. For a fixed-end heat mode, \(u_t=\alpha u_{xx}\) gives \(\rho_n=\alpha(n\pi/L)^2\) for mode \(n\), so the first-mode rate is linear in diffusivity \(\alpha\). For the damped pendulum near its downward equilibrium, \(\ddot\theta+ b/(mL^2)\dot\theta+(g/L)\sin\theta=0\), the underdamped envelope decays at \(\rho=b/(2mL^2)\). These are exponent-one laws in their respective dissipation parameters, not evidence of one universal bifurcation exponent. At zero pendulum damping the equilibrium is a conservative center, not an asymptotically recovering stable state.

Phase 48 studied the dimensionless fixed-end path diffusion equation with \(N\) interior nodes, spacing \(h=1/(N+1)\), and unit diffusion. The discrete modal rate is \(\lambda_{N,n}=4h^{-2}\sin^2(n\pi h/2)\), while the continuum fixed-end rate is \((n\pi)^2\). Their ratio is \([\sin(n\pi h/2)/(n\pi h/2)]^2\); at fixed mode its leading relative grid error is second order in \(h\). This is an exact spectral comparison for the specified operator, separate from numerical ODE integration error.

## C. Main findings

**Phase 48: a narrow second-order convergence result.** For modes 1–3 and path sizes \(N=7,15,31,63,127\), reported fitted refinement orders were 1.99942, 1.99767, and 1.99476. Across 60 integrations, the maximum fitted-rate relative error against the exact discrete eigenvalue was \(4.14\times10^{-11}\). The eigenvalue comparison and the integration check answer different questions: the former compares discrete and continuum operators; the latter checks numerical trajectories against the discrete reference. Source: [Phase 48 report](phase_48_pinned_path_convergence.md), with `reports/phase_48_pinned_path_convergence/` outputs.

**Phases 49–50: different laws under different models.** Phase 49 reported fitted exponents 1.000000 for heat and 1.000029 for the pendulum in their own chosen parameters. The pendulum rate fit’s maximum relative error is reported at approximately \(7.6\times10^{-5}\); source summaries differ in the last rounded digit (7.60 vs 7.61), so this brief retains only the approximate value. These are model-specific checks, not a shared mechanism. Phase 50 compared a fold-derived predictor with an exponent-one baseline on held-out data generated from the canonical fold form: mean absolute log-rate error was 0.0040102 versus 0.9184049. The fold generator supplied the relationship being tested; the benchmark used known generator structure and margin, and did not validate a control plant. See [Phase 49](phase_49_critical_slowing_down.md), [Phase 50](phase_50_cross_domain_validation.md), and `reports/phase_50_cross_domain_validation/`.

**Phases 53–55: classification, rate estimation, and exponent identifiability diverged.** At Phase 53’s primary setting, model-informed classification was correct on 589/600 cases (3 false classifications, 8 indeterminate); the model-agnostic calibrated feature baseline was correct on 586/600 (0 false, 14 indeterminate). Separately, the all-class exponent criterion failed. For transcritical, only 26/40 scaling groups were valid; the estimated boundary mean was 0.9689 (truth 1), while the equilibrium exponent estimate was 1.228 (95% seed-bootstrap interval 1.078–1.364) against theoretical 1. Thus classification success did not establish exponent identifiability. The baseline calibration used the same five settings as evaluation, limiting claims of generalization to unseen settings. See [Phase 53 report](phase_53_recovery_scaling_identifiability.md) and `reports/phase_53_recovery_scaling/`.

Phase 54 varied amplitude, noise, observation window, and supplied equilibrium/boundary information in a paired synthetic factorial design. Under its reference-information condition, the frozen exponent criterion passed 3/18 fold, 4/18 transcritical, and 8/18 pitchfork cells; 3,840/4,320 groups were valid. Higher noise generally worsened validity and exponent errors; longer windows and larger tested perturbations generally helped. Oracle-parameter conditions are diagnostics, not deployable performance. Phase 55 then evaluated 10,500 held-out trajectories at five sub-5% amplitudes with paired zero-noise and noisy arms. No sub-5% amplitude passed its exponent-identifiability criterion. At a fixed intermediate margin, noisy relative-rate-error amplitude slopes were −1.314 (fold), −20.697 (transcritical), and −6.027 (pitchfork), while deterministic signal-to-noise slopes were 1.253, 1.245, and 1.238. Zero-noise rate-error slopes were small and positive: 0.0113, 0.0225, and 0.0338. This is consistent with a substantial low-SNR contribution and a smaller deterministic finite-amplitude/model-fit signature under that design; it does not uniquely apportion causation. Phase 55’s noise-scale initialization depended on the true equilibrium-derived scale, a limitation for a fully blind observation-only estimator. See [Phase 54](phase_54_exponent_identifiability_ablation.md), its [protocol](phase_54_exponent_identifiability_ablation_protocol.md), [Phase 55](phase_55_sub5_percent_perturbation.md), its [protocol](phase_55_sub5_percent_perturbation_protocol.md), and the corresponding report directories.

**Phase 57: noise dose and estimator form.** The held-out evaluation contained 6,000 traces and 18,000 fit attempts on a fixed 75-second grid (751 samples at 0.1-second spacing), with 8 calibration and 50 evaluation seeds. At amplitude 0.0025 and noise dose 0.05 of equilibrium, free-offset exponential mean relative absolute rate error was 2.61 (fold), 21.95 (transcritical), and 7.40 (pitchfork); numerical fit validity was 100%, which does not imply accurate rates. Tail-offset fits were valid for 4,969/6,000 attempts and could be biased or invalid. Only 3/36 nonzero-noise model/amplitude/estimator exponent conditions passed the unchanged Phase 55 criterion: the free-offset estimator at amplitude 0.04 for fold at dose 0.0025 and transcritical at doses 0.0025 and 0.01. No tail-offset condition passed. The exact-form fit performed well on matching synthetic generators but was model-informed and is not an equal-information model-agnostic competitor. Dose-by-estimator performance varied by model and amplitude; no universal winner emerged. See [Phase 57 report](phase_57_noise_dose_estimator_comparison.md), [protocol](phase_57_noise_dose_estimator_comparison_protocol.md), and `reports/phase_57_noise_dose_estimator_comparison/`.

## D. Interpretation and limitations

The demonstrated results are mathematical derivations under explicit assumptions, numerical checks against specified references, and controlled synthetic outcomes. Plausible interpretations include that signal-to-noise limits small-perturbation rate fitting and that finite-amplitude dynamics can affect an exponential fit. The paired Phase 55 comparison and Phase 57 dose sweep support those interpretations within their generators, but do not uniquely identify mechanisms in other systems.

The sequence leaves several questions open. Its synthetic observations use specified noise and observation windows; they do not encompass process noise, drift, sensor dynamics, nonstationarity, model mismatch, branch misidentification, or unknown external forcing. Recovery-rate error can be very large even when an optimizer reports a valid fit. Rate accuracy, classification confusion, and exponent validity/coverage have separate denominators and criteria. Model-informed estimators and oracle-parameter conditions must remain distinguished from observation-only performance. The fixed absolute Phase 57 grid avoids encoding true rate in tau-scaled timestamps, but represents different effective durations in units of each system’s recovery time.

No universal estimator winner, uniquely decomposed error mechanism, physical equivalence, real-world predictive performance, or financial utility is established. The equations encode the scaling laws in the synthetic generators. Independent empirical validation would require a named, measured target; appropriate mechanism and operating-point evidence; permitted, traceable observations; and a prespecified held-out test. Phase 52 found no qualified engineering target (`NO-GO`), and Phase 32 remains `BLOCKED_AUTHORIZATION`. No market data or trading claim is part of this thread.

## F. Research conclusion

The thread contributes a traceable set of branch-specific recovery laws, a narrow second-order discrete-to-continuum convergence result, and controlled evidence that classification, rate estimation, and exponent identifiability can succeed or fail separately under finite noisy observation. It also shows that noise dose and estimator form interact in model-dependent ways on the tested normal forms. The scientific problem is not solved: external disturbances, measurement processes, model mismatch, and independent targets remain untested. Phase 58 therefore closes the current synthetic sequence for now. Reopen it only for a materially distinct falsification question or a qualified, permitted measured system. Phase 52 remains `NO-GO`; Phase 32 remains `BLOCKED_AUTHORIZATION`.
